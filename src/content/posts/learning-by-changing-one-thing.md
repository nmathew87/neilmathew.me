When everything changes at once, it is hard to tell which change mattered.

A smaller experiment is easier to read. Start with something that works, change one part, and look carefully at the result.

That might mean adjusting the spacing on a page, replacing a function, or trying a different way to explain an idea. The size of the change matters less than being able to understand its effect.

A useful loop:

1. Write down what you expect to happen.
2. Change one thing.
3. Compare the result with your expectation.
4. Keep a note of what surprised you.

The note is often the valuable part. It turns a passing observation into something you can return to—and sometimes into the beginning of the next experiment.
